In my memory, and maybe yours, childhood was filled with _Magic: the Gathering_. Wild neverending games late into the night—or at least until bedtime. Shuffling cards on the floor of a Boy Scout camp cabin.

Looking back, it seems like an eternity. Recall your weekly visits to the neighborhood game shop, tearing open booster packs. Did you, like me, salivate over the Black Lotus (p. [X](#black-lotus)) in the glass case, trying to figure out why it was so powerful?

Decades later, I was surprised to work out that my friends and I collected and played _Magic_ for only little more than a year, starting around July 1994. Perhaps as you piece together your own timeline, you'll find the same astonishing dilation.

How is it possible, I now wonder, that a mere year inside the magical world of Urza and Mishra could consume one's childhood memories like this?

✧ ✧ ✧

If you're like me, you've kept a shoebox full of your treasured cardboard relics on a closet shelf ever since. Maybe you, too, dust them off every so often to jam games using half-remembered rules with your oldest friends. For an hour you're a kid again, lost in that magical world.

You may be as surprised as I was to learn that there's a thriving, growing global community of wizards just like you, enamored of these artifacts from our past. There's never been a better time to dive back into the nostalgic land of Old School (OS) Magic, as it is called. OS tournaments are held every month all around the world. And while expensive cards like the "Power Nine" (see p. [X](#formats)) are featured in many winning decks, you will also see familiar faces from your own arsenal of yesteryear: Savannah Lions (p. [X](#savannah-lions)), Lightning Bolt (p. [X](#lightning-bolt)), Giant Growth (p. [X](#giant-growth)).

These early _Magic_ cards, from Alpha (Aug 1993) to Fallen Empires (Nov 1994), represent a special moment in gaming history. By modern-day standards, the $card_count cards range from wildly underpowered to, some would say, broken. But the raw genius of game creator Richard Garfield, art director Jesper Myrfors, and their small band of friends produced a creative torrent so rich and full of joy that this short stack of cards continues to captivate players old and new even today. While this set of cards will never change, frozen in time, there are always new discoveries to be had: tricky combinations, diamonds in the rough, new ways of looking at cards once considered unimportant.

My hope with this book is to reawaken memories of casting your own first _Magic_ spells on the sidewalk or kitchen table. It's time to dig your cards out of the closet. Flip through the pages ahead to reacquaint yourself with the fearsome creatures and wicked enchantments you always wished you had—they're probably cheaper than you think. Or if the game is new to you, it's never too late to start. Throw together a couple decks, enlist a friend, and clear off a spot on the carpet: it's time for _Magic_.

✧ ✧ ✧

I invite you to flip through the pages of this book and let your imagination run wild; they fill me with the thrill of discovery just as these cards did decades ago. On one page, you'll find an offbeat synergy; on another, a novel answer to a bothersome threat. You'll find that the card names, iconic art, and flavor text conjure a mighty world both deeply familiar and somehow always new.

I have come to think of these first glimmers from the dawn of _Magic_ as an artifact to be treasured—and played, again and again, late into the night. Every card has a story, and each has a place of honor in a deck you just haven't gotten around to building yet.

Andy Rossmeissl, 5 December 2025
