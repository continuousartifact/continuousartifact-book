If you are reading this it means you are on the threshold of starting your journey into Old School (OS) _Magic_. Welcome! Read on for tips and tricks on how to build up your collection, but remember: each journey is unique and so is yours.

Starting an OS collection from scratch can be an expensive endeavor but does not have to break the bank if planned correctly. The first step is to investigate the card pool—the book you’re holding is perfect for that, and you can find a list of legal sets on page [X](#formats). Most OS communities allow reprints using the same art and frame. This is a crucial detail in building up your collection, because reprint versions of cards are often more affordable. The main reprint sets to look at are: Revised (3ED), Fourth Edition (4ED) and Chronicles (CHR). All three sets offer reprints of popular staples such as Braingeyser (3ED, p. [X](#braingeyser)), Land Tax (4ED, p. [X](#land-tax)), and Erhnam Djinn (CHR, p. [X](#erhnam-djinn)). Also consider the foreign language reprints, referred to as FBB & FWB (Foreign Black/White Bordered), and the oft-forgotten “time-shifted” cards in the set Time Spiral. Any card in this book with a valid reprint will have a ↻ symbol below it, and the index lists all reprints for each card.

Before we jump into the staples, I would first like to have a look at the mana base. Every deck needs a good mana base. It’s no surprise, then, that dual lands have been the currency of _Magic_ since its inception. However, my advice is not to begin with the dual lands, but instead look at the more affordable options. A good starting point is City of Brass (p. [X](#city-of-brass)). City of Brass is a land that can provide every color of mana, and the CHR printing should not set you back too much. Another great card to strengthen your mana base is Fellwar Stone (p. [X](#fellwar-stone)), a mana rock that has been re-printed in 4ED. The reason Fellwar is such a good mana fixer is because a lot of OS decks play with dual lands and multiple copies of City of Brass. As soon as your opponent has a City out, your Fellwar can produce any color of mana. A decent copy should only cost a couple of bucks. My last tip in the mana department is also the most obvious one, Sol Ring (p. [X](#sol-ring)). Sol Ring got a reprint in 3ED making it an affordable auto-include in most decks.

Now that we have investigated the mana base it is time to look at the staples. Staples are cards that are considered powerful, versatile cards that can be included in a wide variety of decks. The good news is that most OS staples have been reprinted and therefore do not have to cost you an arm and a leg. Let’s go through the reprinted staples per color starting with white. **White**, the color of justice and order, has the best removal reprints with Swords to Plowshares (p. [X](#swords-to-plowshares)), Disenchant (p. [X](#disenchant)), and Balance (p. [X](#balance)), and one of the best creatures in the format, Serra Angel (p. [X](#serra-angel)). **Blue**, the color of tricks and permission, offers us powerful spells like Counterspell (p. [X](#counterspell)), Control Magic (p. [X](#control-magic)), and Serendib Efreet (p. [X](#serendib-efreet)), all printed in 3ED. **Black**, the color of power and self-interest, has staples such as Hypnotic Specter (p. [X](#hypnotic-specter)), Dark Ritual (p. [X](#dark-ritual)), Demonic Tutor (p. [X](#demonic-tutor)), and the scary Mind Twist (p. [X](#mind-twist)) up for grabs in the reprint sets. **Red**, the color of chaos, brings us reprints of the infamous Shivan Dragon (p. [X](#shivan-dragon)), the efficient Lightning Bolt (p. [X](#lightning-bolt)), and the powerful Blood Moon (p. [X](#blood-moon)). **Green**, the color of nature and growth, has the best re-prints of ramp creatures in the game with Llanowar Elves (p. [X](#llanowar-elves)) and Birds of Paradise (p. [X](#birds-of-paradise)). It also offers the instant pump spell Giant Growth (p. [X](#giant-growth)) and the restricted Regrowth (p. [X](#regrowth)). These are just a few examples of the staples that have been reprinted. I have added a list of ten useful reprints per color that you can get to kick off your collection, including the best reprinted staples on the next spread.

After reading this, you might think that starting up an OS collection is all about getting the useful reprints, but the truth is far from it. You get the cheaper reprints so you have the budget to get your hands on your next “chase card.” A piece of the Power 9 (p. [X](#formats)), perhaps, or something more modest like an alpha Prodigal Sorcerer (p. [X](#prodigal-sorcerer))? Either way, happy hunting!
